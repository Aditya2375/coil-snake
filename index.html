More information.
A different way to learn.

The first version used Double DQN on 11 features. This version keeps it intact as a baseline and adds a full-board policy learned by imitation and dataset aggregation, not a larger reinforcement-learning claim.

All learning code is NumPy, including the forward pass, cross-entropy gradients and Adam updates. The browser uses exported weights directly. No ML framework, API key or GPU is needed.

The teacher is an algorithm, not a neural network

For each legal relative move, the teacher flood-fills the visible board with the departing tail freed. It prefers tail-reachable positions with enough space, then shorter paths to food. If no candidate meets the safety filter, it prefers the largest reachable region. These are approximate one-step safety heuristics, not a proof against future traps.

The board-aware network imitates this teacher but receives no rescue or action masking during inference. The teacher menu option uses the algorithm directly and is labeled classical. Human turns use neither.

Training and selection

Initial demonstrations include 4% random exploratory moves. In five subsequent DAgger collections, the learner visits new training positions; the teacher labels them, and 15% of executed training moves follow the teacher. At evaluation, only the learner's argmax is used. A replay cap of 100,000 positions and 256-example batches limit memory.

Development seeds 31000–31039 monitor rounds without selecting a checkpoint. Seeds 32000–32199 select among three final candidates. The final 1,000-game test is 40000–40999, never used for training or model selection. Training game seeds are disjoint. The original published 100-seed evaluation remains in its baseline checkpoint.

What remains unsolved

The neural policy still loses on body traps, changes under small numerical differences, and can be worse than the original on individual seeds. The teacher can also trap itself. A small 8×8 benchmark does not establish general game intelligence or performance on larger boards. Scores are neither probabilities nor a safety guarantee.

Privacy/security: static files only. No uploads, accounts, storage, analytics or remote inference. Model and worker files are same-origin. Game seeds are plain local inputs. Screens and exported results contain no private data.

Method context: Mnih et al. on DQN. Ross et al. on DAgger. These are educational implementations, not reproductions of paper-scale results.
